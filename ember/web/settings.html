Heating

from Hive —

Ember signs in to Hive with your own email and password, so there is nothing to copy around and no probe to remember to run. If Hive sends you a two-step code, it appears here and you type it in — the phone is the only place that code exists.

or sign in on the Hive app first, then press the button

Your password stays on this machine and is never sent back to the browser, so the box is empty every time. If a password is already saved, leave it empty and press the button — Ember will use the one it has.

Gas

from your Octopus meter —

Give Ember your Octopus API key and it works out the rest: the account, the meter point, every meter on it, the tariff in force and the rates. Nothing else to type.

Where the house is

for the weather

A postcode is enough. Ember looks it up in the UK's own postcode directory and turns it into a latitude and longitude. It needs no key of its own, and it does not mind whether you type PO4 0PL or PO40PL. A town name works too.

Numbers

how Ember works them out
the calorific value is on your gas bill · the unit is worked out from the meter and the readings, and Ember says which it used

Display

Stored history

—

Ember keeps a small local database so it can show heating hours, compare the week and fit the forecast. It does not grow forever: the gas readings and the heating polls are kept for — days, and the log of which room was asking for heat for — days. Anything older is deleted once a day.

On this machine

Everything Ember knows is in two files in the data directory, and the secrets in them are readable only by you. Ember never sends your password or API key to the browser — this page only ever sees what you type into it.
