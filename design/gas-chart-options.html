Ember — what the gas chart could be

The 24-hour bar chart is the wrong shape for this data. Octopus delivers gas a day or two late, so the last 24 hours is usually empty or nearly so — and on the days when it is not empty, it is showing a day that ended two days ago. Three alternatives, drawn with the real numbers: 2.43 m³ a day, a meter exchange on 3 November, and today with no reading at all.

A  ·  A week of days

One column per day for the last seven days, height = that day's total, with a line for the weekly average and today's estimate in plum. It is always full, it is the unit people think in (a day's gas), and it sits next to the "7-day average" and "vs average" tiles without repeating them. A day with no readings is drawn as a gap, never as a zero — and the meter exchange reads as what it is.

B  ·  The shape of a day

The same 24 hours as the old chart, but each column is the average use in that hour across the last week, not one night's readings. It is never empty, it answers a question the old chart could not — when does this house use gas — and it makes the cold morning and the warm evening visible at a glance. Today's actual figure, or its estimate, is marked.

C  ·  The month

A calendar grid of the last 30 days, each day shaded by how much gas it used. It carries the month budget, it shows the meter exchange as a visible hole rather than a mystery, and it is the view that answers "is this month normal?" — which is the question a 24-hour chart never could. This was the direction chosen in the design; the missing piece was ever having the history to draw it.
